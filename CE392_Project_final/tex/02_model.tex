\section{Model and Numerical Design}
\label{sec:model}

Before any hardware is built, the model topology and the numerical format must be fixed. Both decisions are constraints that the hardware datapath is then specialised around.

\subsection{MobileFaceNet Topology}
\label{sec:model:topology}

We target the original MobileFaceNet topology~\cite{chen2018mobilefacenetsefficientcnnsaccurate} with a square $3\times112\times112$ input and a $7\times7$ global depthwise convolution, as also adopted by the Eyeriss-style accelerator in~\cite{7738524}. The complete layer schedule is shown in Table~\ref{tab:mobile}. When each Bottleneck is expanded into its three constituent sub-layers (PW1, DW, PW2), the full model comprises \textbf{50 layers} (L0--L49).

The recurring spatial dimensions across the network are $H = W \in \{7, 14, 28, 56\}$ and the channel dimensions are $C_\text{in}, C_\text{out} \in \{64, 128, 256, 512\}$. These ranges drive the array-size choice in Section~\ref{sec:hw:frontend}.

\subsection{Quantization}
\label{sec:model:precision}

The reference model is trained in INT8 via the QuantFace flow~\cite{9955645} and the hardware mirrors that representation directly. All weights, activations, and partial sums use 8-bit fixed-point storage, with 32-bit accumulators inside each MAC. Weights are stored with a symmetric scheme ($\textit{zp}_w = 0$) and activations with an asymmetric scheme (non-zero $\textit{zp}_a$). The MAC operation expands as

\begin{equation}
(act - zp_a)(wgt - zp_w) = act \cdot wgt - zp_w \cdot act - zp_a \cdot wgt + zp_a \cdot zp_w.
\label{eq:quant}
\end{equation}

Because $\textit{zp}_w = 0$ for symmetric weights, the last two terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:quant} vanish; the runtime cost of asymmetric activation reduces to one $-zp_a \cdot wgt$ correction per output channel, which is absorbed into the bias and applied once per layer. The final hardware bit-widths are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:fixed_point}.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Quantized Hardware Datatypes}
\label{tab:fixed_point}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & \textbf{Scheme} & \textbf{Width} \\
\midrule

\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The accuracy of this INT8 representation against the floating-point QuantFace baseline is presented in Section~\ref{sec:verify:flow}, alongside the broader face-verification benchmark suite.
