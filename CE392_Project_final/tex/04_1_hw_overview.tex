\subsection{Architecture Overview}
\label{sec:hw:overview}

Both implementations share the same algorithmic specification (MobileFaceNet INT16 Q10, 50-layer sequential execution, residual shortcuts, mixed standard / depthwise / pointwise convolutions) but realise the compute and memory subsystems differently to suit the target technology. Table~\ref{tab:asic_vs_fpga} contrasts the two paths.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{ASIC vs FPGA Implementation Comparison}
\label{tab:asic_vs_fpga}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l X X}
\toprule
\headrow & \textbf{ASIC path (Liao)} & \textbf{FPGA path (Zhang)} \\
\midrule
Technology      & FreePDK45 (45\,nm)              & \\
Compute fabric  & 12$\times$12 = 144-MAC output-stationary systolic array & \\
Activation memory & OpenRAM SRAM macro (192-bit $\times$ 64, 1RW) & \\
Partial-sum memory & OpenRAM SRAM macro (40-bit $\times$ 512, 1RW+1R) & \\
Weight storage  & Combinational ROM (synthesised mux tree) & \\
Target clock    & 5.0\,ns (200\,MHz)              & \\
Achieved metric & 14.5\,FPS, 96.3\,mW             & \\
Verification    & RTL + Genus syn + Innovus post-route + SDF & \\
Implementation tools & Cadence Genus + Innovus     & \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The two implementations are complementary: the ASIC path quantifies the lower bound on power and the upper bound on frequency achievable in 45\,nm silicon, while the FPGA path provides on-board functional validation and rapid bring-up.
